# House rules: IT

Decisions that hold for **every** Italian text. Glosses and translations
are written in **{{yourLanguage}}**. This list grows.

## Base forms

1. Verbs carry the **infinitive**: `va` → `andare`, `esco` → `uscire`.
2. `essere` and `avere` are **AUX** when they carry a participle
   (`ho visto`, `è andata`) and **VERB** when they stand alone.
3. Names: base form as written (`Marco`, `Roma`), the gloss is the name.
4. Plural shares the key with the singular: `libri` → `it:libro:NOUN`.
   This includes the irregular ones: `uomini` → `it:uomo:NOUN`,
   `braccia` → `it:braccio:NOUN`.
5. Feminine forms share the key with the masculine: `ragazza` and
   `ragazzo` both give `it:ragazzo:NOUN`.
6. **Determiners take the base form of their own series.** `lo`, `la`,
   `l'`, `i`, `gli`, `le` all give `it:il:DET` - the article is chosen by
   the sound that follows, and that is one word with many shapes, not
   many words.

## Where Italian pulls words together

7. **Articulated prepositions stay one word**, and the gloss opens them
   up: `del` → "of the", `nella` → "in the", `agli` → "to the".
8. **Attached pronouns are their own words**: `darmelo` is `dare` + `mi`
   + `lo`, `alzati` is `alza` + `ti`.
9. Elisions with an apostrophe are words of their own: `l'amico` is
   `l'` + `amico`, and `l'` gives `it:il:DET`.

## Glossing

10. Articles follow the **Italian** gender: `la casa` → "the(f) house".
11. `ci` and `ne` are glossed for what they replace, not left out - they
    are where Italian hides a whole phrase in two letters.
12. Where a single word does not exist, hyphenate.

## Phrases

13. Polite formulas, grammatical constructions, fixed terms:
    `per favore`, `di niente`, `c'è` and `ci sono`, `andare a`,
    `stare per`, `avere voglia di`, `va bene`.
14. The base form of a phrase is the citation form, lower case.

## Spelling

15. **Double consonants are part of the word**: `nono` and `nonno` are
    different words with different keys. Never normalise them.
