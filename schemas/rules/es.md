# House rules: ES

Decisions that hold for **every** Spanish text. Glosses and translations
are written in **{{yourLanguage}}**. This list grows.

## Base forms

1. Verbs carry the **infinitive**: `busca` → `buscar`, `está` → `estar`.
2. `ser`, `estar` and `haber` are **AUX** when they carry another verb
   (`está comiendo`, `ha llegado`) and **VERB** when they stand alone
   (`es médico`, `está en casa`). Decide by the sentence, not the word.
3. Names: base form as written (`María`, `Sevilla`), the gloss is the name.
4. Plural shares the key with the singular: `libros` → `es:libro:NOUN`.
5. Feminine forms share the key with the masculine: `profesora` and
   `profesor` both give `es:profesor:NOUN`.
6. **Determiners and pronouns take the base form of their own series.**
   `la` is `el`, `las` is `el`, `una` is `uno`, `mis` is `mi` - never the
   noun standing beside them. This is the mistake that happens first.

## Where Spanish pulls words together

7. **Contractions stay one word**, and the gloss opens them up:
   `al` → "to the", `del` → "of the".
8. **Attached pronouns are their own words**: `dármelo` is `dar` + `me` +
   `lo`, `levántate` is `levanta` + `te`. The written accent belongs to
   the verb.
9. Reflexive `se` is **PRON**, not part of the verb: `se llama` is two
   words. The infinitive keeps its `-se` only in the description.

## Glossing

10. Articles follow the **Spanish** gender: `la mesa` → "the(f) table".
11. Hidden grammar becomes visible: the personal `a` (`veo a María`) is
    glossed, not dropped - it marks a person as the object.
12. Where a single word does not exist, hyphenate.

## Phrases

13. Polite formulas, grammatical constructions, fixed terms:
    `por favor`, `de nada`, `hay que`, `tener que`, `acabar de`,
    `ir a` for the near future, `qué tal`, `por supuesto`.
    Ordinary word sequences stay out.
14. The base form of a phrase is the citation form, lower case.

## Spelling

15. The **written accent is part of the word**: `si` and `sí`, `el` and
    `él`, `tu` and `tú` are different words with different keys.
