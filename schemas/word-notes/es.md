# What a Spanish word description contains

Everything is written in the reader's own language; the examples are
Spanish. Two or three **labelled lines**, then at most two sentences.

## The stems to keep in view

Spanish verbs need **three stems**, and a learner who has only the first
cannot form the others:

- the **present stem**, which may change its vowel: `e → ie` (*pensar →
  pienso*), `o → ue` (*poder → puedo*), `e → i` (*pedir → pido*). The
  change happens in the stressed forms only, so `nosotros` keeps the
  plain stem - name the pattern, not just the one form.
- the **preterite stem** of the strong verbs, which is a different word
  altogether: `tuv-`, `estuv-`, `pus-`, `quis-`, `hic-`, `dij-`, `vin-`,
  `sup-`, `pud-`. These take their own unstressed endings.
- the **participle**, irregular in a handful of common verbs: *hecho,
  dicho, visto, puesto, vuelto, escrito, abierto, roto, muerto*.

Name whichever of the three is not predictable from the infinitive.

## Per part of speech

**VERB** — `Infinitivo`; the full `Presente` (all six); `Indefinido` in
the first person at least, more when the stem is strong; `Participio`
when it is irregular. Say which conjugation (-ar, -er, -ir) and name the
stem change as a pattern.

**NOUN** — `Género` shown through both articles (*el libro · un libro*);
`Plural`. Call out the traps: nouns in `-a` that are masculine (*el
problema, el día*), feminine nouns that take *el* in the singular (*el
agua fría*), and nouns whose meaning changes with gender (*el capital /
la capital*).

**ADJ** — `Masculino`, `Femenino`, `Plural`. Say when the adjective has
only two forms (*grande, feliz*), and when it goes before the noun and
changes meaning there (*un gran hombre / un hombre grande*).

**ADV** — whether it is formed with *-mente*, and where it stands.

**PRON** — the series it belongs to: subject, direct object, indirect
object, prepositional, reflexive. One example with its translation.
Mention *leísmo* only where it is the common form.

**DET** — the series *masculino · femenino · plural*, and the
contractions *a + el = al*, *de + el = del*.

**ADP** — one example per meaning with its translation. For *por* and
*para*, always name the other one.

**NUM** — apocope (*un libro*, not *uno*), agreement in the hundreds
(*doscientas casas*).

**CCONJ**, **SCONJ** — what it joins; whether it forces the subjunctive
(*para que, aunque, cuando* with future meaning).

**PART** — what it does, one example.

**INTJ** — when it is said and how formal.

**PHRASE** — the literal words, then when it is used.

## Always

- **Name the partner** where Spanish has a pair a learner must decide
  between: *ser/estar*, *por/para*, *saber/conocer*, *muy/mucho*,
  *pedir/preguntar*.
- **The written accent is information**: it marks the stressed syllable,
  and it tells words apart (*el/él, tu/tú, si/sí, mas/más*). Say so when
  it matters for this word.
- Leave out: the meaning again, examples without a point, textbook prose.
