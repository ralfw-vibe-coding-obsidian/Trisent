# What a Russian word description contains

Everything is written in the reader's own language; the examples are
Russian. Two or three **labelled lines**, then at most two sentences.

## The stems to keep in view

- the **aspect partner**, before anything else: *писать / написать*,
  *читать / прочитать*, *говорить / сказать*. Two words, one meaning,
  different jobs. **Never describe one without naming the other**, and
  say when the pair is not formed by a prefix but is a different word.
- the **present stem** and the **infinitive stem**, which are frequently
  not the same: *писать → пишу*, *ждать → жду*, *мочь → могу*. The past
  tense is built from the infinitive stem, the present from the other -
  a learner who knows one cannot derive the other.
- the **consonant mutation** that comes with it: *с → ш*, *д → ж*, *т →
  ч*, *к → ч*. Where it happens, it happens in a predictable set of
  persons - say which.
- for verbs of motion, the **pair of pairs**: *идти / ходить*, *ехать /
  éздить* - one-directional against habitual, each with its own
  perfective.

## Per part of speech

**VERB** — `Инфинитив`; `Настоящее` with the first and second person at
least, more when the stem mutates; `Прошедшее` in the masculine;
`Вид` naming the aspect and its partner. Say which conjugation (1st or
2nd) and **which case the verb governs** when it is not the accusative
(*помогать* + dative, *бояться* + genitive).

**NOUN** — `Род`; `Р. п.` the genitive singular, because it reveals the
declension; `Мн. ч.` the nominative plural, and the genitive plural when
it is irregular (*мест*, *друзей*). Mark the **stress and whether it
moves** (*окно́ → о́кна*). Say when the noun is animate, because that
changes its accusative.

**ADJ** — the full forms `м/ж/ср/мн`, and the **short form** when it is
in use (*рад, должен, болен*). Say when the adjective is used as a noun.

**ADV** — how it is formed from the adjective, and where it stands.

**PRON** — the series, the **declension**, and the *н-* that appears
after a preposition (*у него*, not *у его*). One example with its
translation.

**DET** — the forms and their agreement.

**ADP** — **which case or cases it takes** - this is the description, not
an afterthought. One example per case with its translation (*в* +
accusative for direction, + prepositional for place).

**NUM** — which case the counted noun takes (*два стола*, *пять столов*),
and that the numeral itself declines.

**CCONJ**, **SCONJ** — what it joins, one example, and whether a comma
is obligatory before it.

**PART** — what it does, one example. *ли*, *бы*, *же*, *-то*, *-нибудь*.

**INTJ** — when it is said and how formal.

**PHRASE** — the literal words, then when it is used.

## Always

- **Mark the stress.** It is not written, it moves within a paradigm, and
  it distinguishes words: *за́мок* (castle) and *замо́к* (lock), *пи́сать*
  and *писа́ть*. Without it a learner cannot say the word at all.
- **Name the aspect partner** for every verb.
- **Name the case** a preposition or verb governs.
- Leave out: the meaning again, examples without a point, textbook prose.
