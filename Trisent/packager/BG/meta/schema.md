# What a Bulgarian word description contains

Everything is written in the reader's own language; the examples are
Bulgarian. Two or three **labelled lines**, then at most two sentences.

## The stems to keep in view

Bulgarian has **no infinitive**, so the base form is the first person
singular present: *чета*, *говоря*. From there, two things must be
learned that cannot be guessed from each other:

- the **present stem**, which decides the conjugation: look at the vowel
  before the ending in the second person - *четеш* (1st), *говориш*
  (2nd), *имаш* (3rd).
- the **aorist stem**, the past stem, which is often a different shape:
  *чета → четох*, *дойда → дойдох*, *взема → взех*. A learner who has
  only the present stem cannot tell a story in the past.

And above both of them:

- the **aspect partner**. Nearly every verb comes as a pair -
  imperfective and perfective, *пиша / напиша*, *чета / прочета*. They
  are two words with one meaning and different jobs. **Never describe one
  without naming the other.**

## Per part of speech

**VERB** — `Сегашно` (1st person, then the full present); `Минало` in
the aorist first person; `Вид` naming the aspect and its partner. Say
which conjugation, and mark the stress.

**NOUN** — `Род` with the indefinite form; `Определено` with the article
**suffixed to the word** (*книга → книгата*, *стол → столът / стола*);
`Мн. ч.` for the plural, which is rarely predictable (*стол → столове*,
*град → градове*, *мъж → мъже*). Two traps worth a sentence: masculine
nouns have a **full and a short article** (*столът* as subject, *стола*
elsewhere), and after a numeral masculine nouns take a **count form**
(*два стола*, not *столове*).

**ADJ** — `М/Ж/Ср` and `Мн. ч.`, plus the **definite form** (*малък →
малкият*). Say when the stem loses a vowel (*малък → малка*).

**ADV** — whether it is the neuter of an adjective, and where it stands.

**PRON** — the series, and above all the **full and short forms** (*мене
/ ме*, *на мене / ми*): which one is used where, with one example. This
is the single most confusing thing for a beginner.

**DET** — the forms, and how the article attaches.

**ADP** — one example per meaning with its translation. Bulgarian has
lost its cases, so prepositions carry the work that endings carry in
Russian - say what the preposition does, not only what it means.

**NUM** — the count form it triggers, and the masculine personal forms
(*двама мъже*).

**CCONJ**, **SCONJ** — what it joins, one example. For *да*: that it
replaces the missing infinitive.

**PART** — what it does, one example. *ли* for questions, *не* for
negation, *се* as the reflexive clitic and where it stands.

**INTJ** — when it is said and how formal.

**PHRASE** — the literal words, then when it is used.

## Always

- **Mark the stress** when it is not where a learner would guess. It is
  not written in Bulgarian, it moves, and it changes words: *вълна*
  (wool) and *вълна́* (wave), *пара* and *пара́*.
- **Name the aspect partner** for every verb, every time.
- **Say where the article goes**, because it is inside the word and the
  gloss has to show it.
- Leave out: the meaning again, examples without a point, textbook prose.
